What is happening in this experience?

In voiced speech, vibrating vocal folds provide a source. The throat, mouth, tongue, and lips help shape which frequencies reach a listener strongly. This simplified example starts with harmonics of 216 Hz and changes their relative strength.

The “ah,” “oh,” and “ee” settings are illustrative models. Actual voices vary with the person, language, accent, pitch, and delivery. There is no single universal frequency for a vowel.

While a generated sound plays, the graph shows its live audio spectrum. When stopped, it shows the calculated components of the current model. In microphone mode, it shows the incoming microphone signal. Rooms, microphones, and device processing affect that display.

The 432 Hz tuning reference sets the musical base note. Higher harmonics extend above it; they help create the sound’s character. The graph does not measure a person’s inner state or identify a spiritual quality.
